Negative-target ideological containers — movements defined by opposition to a single entity rather than commitment to a positive program — function as epistemic traps because their flexibility makes them unfalsifiable and their monocausal structure degrades analytical capacity over time. They can be identified by three markers: monocausal attribution, cross-ideological convergence on a shared enemy, and framework-shifting under challenge. Anti-Zionism is the clearest contemporary example, but anti-Americanism and Russophobia may be others.
