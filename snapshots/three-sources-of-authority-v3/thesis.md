People rely on others' word on three grounds: office (formally constituted and sanctionable), community (chosen by those relying, who can leave), and open challenge (a claim stands because anyone could contest it and it held). An arrangement for relying on others' word is trustworthy to the extent that these three are held by different parties, so that each can check the others. When one party holds all three, its authority is unchecked: a large online platform writes the rules (office), its users are the community, and it hosts the forum where complaints are aired; a state that controls courts, press and exit is the same. Therefore the practical aim for trust across borders is not a new global authority but a separation, like the separation of powers: whoever holds the office should not own the forum, and people should be able to belong to a community that neither controls. This concerns credibility only; it does not make parties keep agreements when breaking them pays, and it serves only people who have access to all three.
