The optimal identity-assurance architecture is a composition of five elements, each taken from the system that has demonstrated it: (1) self-certifying identifier control from KERI/ACDC; (2) codified per-dimension assurance using NIST SP 800-63 levels carrying Osmio IDQA's eight-dimension vector, thresholded by relying parties; (3) liability on whoever exercised the judgment, via bonded notaries/Attestation Officers for discretionary proofing and certified-provider liability for automated checks; (4) plural independent trust-list publishers as in the WebPKI's four browser root programs, extended to hardware certification; (5) appeal under public law when use is de facto mandatory, as in eIDAS 2. The field maps onto these as follows. KERI/ACDC supplies 1. W3C DIDs partially supply 1 (method-dependent). Sovrin attempted 1 via a ledger and collapsed. X.509 fails 1 but WebPKI root programs supply 4. OpenPGP supplies 1 but fails 2 and 3. NIST 800-63-4 supplies 2's level structure but has no enforcement tier. Osmio IDQA supplies 2's per-dimension vector. Notaries and Osmio Attestation Officers supply 3 at the discretionary tier. UK DVS supplies 3 organizationally and 5 statutorily. AAMVA fails 3 and 4. Login.gov fails 3. GLEIF vLEI supplies 1, partly 2 and 3, fails 4. eIDAS 2 supplies 3 and 5, fails 1 and 4. C2PA fails 4. Common Criteria fails 4 at the hardware tier. Aadhaar fails 4 and 5. Worldcoin fails 2, 4 and 5. Platform reputation and credit-bureau KBA fail condition 1 by design. The closest existing compositions are GLEIF vLEI (has 1, 2 partial, 3; missing 4), Osmio/IDQA (has 1 via its X.509 root CA with KERI-compatible design, 2, and 3 on paper; missing 4; 5 undocumented), UK DVS (has 2, 3, 4 partial, 5; missing 1), and eIDAS 2 (has 2, 3, 5; missing 1 and 4). No existing system supplies all five; the maximum observed is three plus partials.
