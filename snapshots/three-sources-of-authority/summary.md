# An authority is a party whose say-so settles a question for other people who do not check it themselves. An authority's standing comes from one of three sources. (1) Office: it was formally set up under law to give that word, and its office-holders can be punished for abuse. (2) Community: the people relying on it chose to. (3) Open challenge: nothing is believed because of who said it; a claim stands because anyone could have challenged it, people did, and it held, as in science. These three are exhaustive and distinct. Each fails alone in a way the other two repair: an office is a single point that can be captured, and is checked by communities' freedom to go elsewhere and by having its decisions open to challenge; a community's word does not carry to distant strangers, and is helped by answerable office-holders and a shared way of settling disputes; open challenge corrects itself only if participants are real and accountable, which office establishes, and free to dissent, which independent communities protect. Therefore trust between strangers at global scale does not need a new global authority; it needs the three sources working together across borders, and such an arrangement would have no power to compel, only the influence of being believed.
