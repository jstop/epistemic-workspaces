Epistemic resolution — the capacity for public discourse to converge on justified conclusions — is substantially facilitated by participants whose identities persist across encounters, because persistent identity provides a primary (though not sole) mechanism by which arguments accumulate across debates, defeaters are tracked, and dialectical progress is preserved. In the absence of robust institutional memory or equivalent structural substitutes, and assuming a baseline of good-faith participation, the loss of persistent identity severely degrades discourse toward repetitive, zero-state exchanges.
