People rely on another party's word on three overlapping grounds: office (formally constituted, with holders who can be sanctioned), community (the people relying chose to, and can leave), and open challenge (a claim stands because anyone could contest it and it held; a proven track record is a form of this, with experience doing the contesting). The grounds blend in practice but differ in how they fail: an office can be captured, a community's word does not carry to distant strangers, and open challenge works only when participants are real, accountable and free to dissent. Each failure is partly mitigated by the other two, though a powerful enough actor could degrade all three at once. For the specific problem of knowing whose word to rely on across borders, no new global authority is needed: the three grounds working together are enough, resting on existing national courts for enforcement. This arrangement does not address compliance problems, where parties accept the facts and still break agreements because it pays; those require consequences that being believed cannot supply.
