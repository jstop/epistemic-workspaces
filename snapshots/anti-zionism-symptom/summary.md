# Anti-Zionism functions as a causal attribution engine for populations and movements that lack strategic exposure — it's a symptom of geopolitical unsophistication, not necessarily its cause. The causal arrow may run the other direction: bad geopolitical thinking produces anti-Zionism as a convenient moral framework, rather than anti-Zionism degrading otherwise good thinking.

## Supporting Arguments

### Full convergence: attribution engine + causal direction + moral convenience + non-degradation → anti-Zionism is symptom not cause
*Pattern: abduction | Confidence: 60%*

- **Claim:** anti-zionism functions-as causal-attribution-engine (70%)
  - Anti-Zionism provides a ready-made explanatory framework that attributes complex geopolitical outcomes to a single identifiable actor (Israel/Zionism), reducing cognitive load for those without deep strategic literacy. It functions as a heuristic that substitutes for genuine causal analysis.
- **Claim:** geopolitical-unsophistication causally-precedes anti-zionist-adoption (55%)
  - The causal arrow runs from pre-existing poor geopolitical reasoning toward adoption of anti-Zionism, not the reverse. Populations with low strategic exposure are drawn to anti-Zionism because it offers moral clarity without requiring regional expertise. The framework is selected for its simplicity, not its explanatory power.
- **Claim:** anti-zionism provides convenient-moral-framework (75%)
  - Anti-Zionism supplies a morally legible narrative (oppressor vs. oppressed) that maps onto pre-existing progressive moral categories. Its convenience lies in collapsing a multi-actor, multi-century conflict into a binary moral schema that requires no specialist knowledge to deploy.
- **Claim:** anti-zionism does-not-degrade otherwise-good-geopolitical-thinking (50%)
  - The thesis's strongest sub-claim: anti-Zionism is not an intellectual toxin that corrupts previously sound analysis, but rather a downstream output of already-degraded analytical capacity. If true, combating anti-Zionism directly is treating symptoms rather than causes.

- **Objection [ACTIVE]:** Bidirectional causation defeater: The thesis assumes a clean one-way causal arrow (bad thinking → anti-Zionism), but ideological frameworks and analytical capacity likely co-constitute each other in feedback loops. Anti-Zionism may begin as a symptom but then actively narrow the analytical repertoire available to its adopters — filtering out disconfirming information, creating social costs for nuance, and structuring attention toward confirming evidence. If so, the symptom/cause distinction collapses into mutual reinforcement, and the thesis's core causal claim is underdetermined by the available evidence. The strongest version of this defeater: even if the initial adoption is downstream of unsophistication, the framework's maintenance and intensification may be genuinely causally upstream of further analytical degradation.
