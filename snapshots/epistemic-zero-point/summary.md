# The zero point of a fallibilist epistemic network is a declared, non-neutral set of hinge commitments: constitutive of the network rather than claims within it, not defeasible inside the network, and revisable only by forking the protocol under a constituted authority. It comprises the initial state of every claim plus the bounds of the space, not the rules of motion within it. The hinge set has four members: (1) Prior — no claim carries default warrant, (2) Ceiling — no claim reaches certainty (Resilience axiom), (3) Floor — signed attestation is indefeasible only relative to the key event log, (4) Entry state — claims enter at full commitment with zero earned entitlement. Declaring these hinges makes the dogmatic stop explicit and accountable, delegating justification to the constituted authority governing the protocol. The network's epistemic humility is itself a hinge, not a theorem.

## Supporting Arguments

### The four declared hinges constitute the network's zero point — they define initial state and bounds of the space, are not defeasible within the network, and are revisable only by protocol fork; this is the best explanation for how a fallibilist network can have a non-circular foundation
*Pattern: abduction | Confidence: 72%*

- **Claim:** prior-hinge entails no-default-warrant (85%)
  - Dung's grounded semantics is the least fixed point from the empty set, so no claim carries default warrant. In gradual semantics the base-score assignment plays this role and must be declared as the network's prior rather than hidden as a parameter. This makes the foundationalist starting point explicit.
- **Claim:** ceiling-hinge enforces no-claim-reaches-certainty (78%)
  - No claim reaches certainty. This is the Resilience axiom of Amgoud and Ben-Naim: an argument with initial weight strictly inside (0,1) never reaches 0 or 1. Enforced either by DF-QuAD with DAG constraint (structural prohibition of coherentist bootstrapping) or by a Resilient continuous dynamical system (e.g., Potyka's quadratic energy model) where bootstrapping is defused arithmetically. The selection between these mechanisms is itself a hinge commitment.
- **Claim:** floor-hinge restricts-indefeasibility-to key-event-log (80%)
  - The signed attestation 'key K signed P at t' is indefeasible only relative to the key event log. Non-repudiation binds a key, not a person. The key-to-person binding is supplied by the identity layer, not the argumentation layer. This separates cryptographic fact from identity claim.
- **Claim:** entry-state-hinge specifies full-commitment-zero-earned-entitlement (82%)
  - A claim enters at full commitment, default entitlement (its base score, formalizing Brandom's prima facie entitlement) and zero earned entitlement, with earned entitlement (unchallenged / challenged / defended / conceded) tracked on a deontic ledger separate from graded strength. This dual-track design separates normative standing from epistemic weight.
- **Claim:** hinge-declaration makes-explicit dogmatic-stop-without-self-grounding (75%)
  - Declaring these hinges does not resolve the Münchhausen trilemma's dogmatic horn. It makes the dogmatic stop explicit and accountable, and relocates the question of its justification to the constituted authority that governs the protocol. The argumentation layer's zero point is not self-grounding: its regress terminates wherever the identity and governance layer's does, and this thesis delegates that terminus rather than supplying it. The network's epistemic humility is itself a hinge, not a theorem of the network.

- **Objection [answered]:** The thesis claims to make the dogmatic stop 'explicit and accountable' by declaring hinges, but this may be a distinction without epistemic difference. If declaring a dogmatic stop is itself a dogmatic act, then explicitness adds only procedural transparency, not epistemic legitimacy. The Münchhausen trilemma is not dissolved but repackaged: the regress is relocated to the governance layer, which faces the same trilemma. The thesis acknowledges this delegation but frames it as a feature; a critic could argue it is merely a deferral that inherits all the original problems. Explicitness is a pragmatic virtue, not an epistemic one — making an unjustified stop visible does not make it justified.
  - *Response:* The objection restates the thesis and calls it a weakness. The thesis says explicitly that declaring the hinges does not resolve the trilemma and does not justify the stop; it claims the stop is declared and accountable. "Explicitness is a pragmatic virtue, not an epistemic one" is hinge epistemology's own position: hinges are not known and not justified, they are constitutive of the practice, and their status is pragmatic. So the objection agrees with the thesis on every point of substance and disagrees only about whether that is worth calling a gain. It is, for a reason the objection misses. There are two different questions in play: whether the stop is justified, and whether the network's dependence on the stop is known. The trilemma settles the first negatively for every architecture. The second is a genuine epistemic property of the network, not of the hinge: an agent who knows which unjustified commitments its conclusions rest on is in a different epistemic state from one who does not, and a hidden parameter and a declared hinge differ exactly there. Declared hinges make dependence auditable, challengeable at the governance layer, and forkable; hidden ones make it none of those. "Making an unjustified stop visible does not make it justified" is true and conceded; it makes the stop's role in every downstream conclusion knowable, which is the epistemic difference the objection says does not exist.

Wittgenstein's treatment of mathematical propositions in On Certainty makes the same move: proofs function as rules of the practice, not as empirical claims that accumulate support. The dependency is real and should be declared as part of the semantics hinge, which the thesis's account of hinges already requires: anything the network cannot revise from inside, including its solver, is declared rather than hidden.
